# Retire on Your Own Terms

The National Pension Scheme (NPS) is a government-backed, voluntary retirement savings scheme regulated by the PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority). It is designed to help individuals build a disciplined retirement corpus through market-linked, professionally managed pension funds — while offering some of the most attractive tax benefits available in India today.

# What is NPS

NPS is a defined-contribution pension scheme where your contributions are invested across a mix of equity, corporate debt, and government securities, based on your chosen allocation or age-based auto allocation. The corpus accumulates over your working years and is used to provide you a regular income after retirement, along with a lump-sum withdrawal.

NPS is open to all Indian citizens (resident and NRI) between 18 and 70 years of age, and is available through two account types:

## Tier I Account (Pension Account)
- The primary retirement account with a lock-in until retirement (age 60).
- Mandatory for availing tax benefits.
- Partial withdrawals permitted only for specific purposes (higher education, medical treatment, home purchase, etc.) after 3 years, subject to conditions.

## Tier II Account (Investment Account)
- A voluntary savings account with no lock-in — withdraw anytime.
- Can only be opened if you already have a Tier I account.
- No tax benefit on contributions (except for specified government employees under a 3-year lock-in option).

# Choice of Pension Funds & Asset Allocation

- Choose from Active Choice (set your own mix of Equity, Corporate Bonds, Government Securities, and Alternative Assets) or Auto Choice (allocation automatically adjusted based on your age and risk appetite).
- Select from PFRDA-registered pension fund managers based on track record and consistency.
- Equity exposure is capped, giving NPS a more measured risk profile than pure equity investments.

# Tax Benefits under NPS

- Deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 under Section 80CCD(1), within the overall Section 80C limit.
- Additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B) — over and above the 80C limit.
- Employer contributions (up to prescribed limits) are deductible under Section 80CCD(2), applicable under both tax regimes.
- On maturity, 60% of the corpus can be withdrawn tax-free; the remaining 40% must be used to purchase an annuity.

# Who Should Consider NPS

- Salaried and self-employed individuals looking to build a dedicated retirement corpus.
- Investors seeking additional tax deduction beyond the ₹1.5 lakh limit under Section 80C.
- Long-term investors comfortable with a lock-in until retirement in exchange for disciplined, low-cost, professionally managed savings.

# Withdrawal & Exit Rules

- At retirement (60 years): Up to 60% of the corpus withdrawn tax-free; the balance is annuitized for regular pension income.
- Before retirement: Full exit permitted only under specific conditions, with a minimum portion mandatorily annuitized.
- Partial withdrawal: Up to 25% of self-contributions, permitted after 3 years for defined purposes, limited to 3 withdrawals over the tenure.
